| **Occupation** | Battle medic, healing magic, ordinary rank |
| **Ability** | Healing magic; cannot fight; opens portals via her phone |

### The origin wound

She was an **ordinary battle medic** — not C-class, unremarkable, the person
present and not in the way. She heals because healing is what she is for. She
does not fight, does not stand out, and is not looked at twice.

When a standard raid goes wrong, her forces scatter and flee in every direction.
Separated from her comrades, an ordinary medic in open country, she has no way
to survive on skill. She believes she will simply die there — **small, abandoned,
unseen**.

That moment is the engine of everything she does. It is not the reason she
becomes good, and it is not the reason she becomes sympathetic. It is the reason
she wants to be the most powerful woman in the world.

### What she wants

Not to be a hero. Not to save a world. Not to be left alone.

**She wants to be the most powerful woman in the world**, as compensation for the
moment she felt smallest and most abandoned — the moment she was an ordinary
person in a place where ordinary people die.

She does not want to be a warm body in the army back home. She does not want to
be the saintess of such a dowdy small kingdom. Both are positions she is
occupying, and positions can be improved on.

### Her relationship to faith

Being the only healer and the kingdom's saintess is **a step toward that goal,
not the destination**. She is using a faith she did not ask for as
infrastructure toward a power she does intend to take.

Her healing is what made her useful enough to be recruited, and what makes her
valuable enough to be worshipped. The same trait is both the reason she was
taken and the reason she is worth taking.

### Why she is dangerous rather than tragic

The story is not about a woman who wanted power and got more of it than she
expected. It is about a woman who found the need itself **manufactured by the
worst moment of her life**, and who has since been feeding it.

She holds the door open. She knows exactly what walks through it, into two places
at once, neither of which asked for it.

## Open questions

### How evil is the player?

She is the protagonist, so **the player is holding the phone** that opens the
breaches. Does the game conceal the cost of each crossing, or show it plainly
and let the player keep doing it?

This is the central design decision, and unlike a traditional otome the player's
actions are **complicit rather than innocent**. The routes are the natural place
to stage it: what she does while the player is watching is the whole game.

### The mask mechanics

How does the game signal that a line is the saint speaking and not Seoyun?

- Deniable phrasing that reads as either
- Second-person interjections in the saint's voice
- A visual tell the player can learn
- Nothing at all — pure reader suspicion

A signal the player can learn to read turns the deception into a **game
mechanic** rather than a late twist.

### What do the love interests know?

In otome the male leads are the reader's proxy, and they are the natural place
to plant doubt. Do they suspect the saint is a fraud, a monster-keeper, or the
person opening the breaches? A route where one of them works it out changes that
route's endgame.

### The magic alliance

Not a character, but an institution that acts as one. Forcibly recruits
everyone with magical power, ranks S through F, and **abandoned an ordinary
medic on a failed raid**. Whether it becomes an antagonist depends on the
